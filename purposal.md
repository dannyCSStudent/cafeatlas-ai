# CafeAtlas AI
## Business Proposal and Launch Plan

Prepared for: Prospective business partner
Prepared: September 2026
Status: Planning proposal; financial figures are estimates for discussion

---

## 1. Executive Summary

CafeAtlas AI is a premium discovery and commerce platform focused on Mexican coffee. The business will combine a curated coffee marketplace with origin stories, producer profiles, educational content, personalized recommendations, and a modern shopping experience across web and mobile.

The opportunity is to build more than a coffee store. Customers should be able to discover where a coffee comes from, understand how it tastes, learn how to brew it, buy it, and continue their relationship with the producers and the CafeAtlas community.

The first commercial version should launch with a focused catalog, reliable fulfillment, and disciplined customer acquisition. It should not begin by importing a large amount of inventory or attempting to serve every market. The recommended approach is:

1. Launch with 4 to 6 carefully selected Mexican coffees.
2. Use a qualified US importer, distributor, or roasting partner while direct-import relationships are developed.
3. Sell whole-bean coffee through one-time purchases, bundles, subscriptions, and gift sets.
4. Use CafeAtlas technology to create differentiation through origin data, storytelling, recommendations, and customer retention.
5. Prove repeat purchasing and unit economics before expanding inventory or geography.

The initial target is approximately $250,000 to $350,000 in first-year revenue, with a lean launch budget of approximately $50,000 to $90,000. These are planning targets, not guarantees. A stronger capitalization target of $75,000 to $125,000 would provide better inventory flexibility and operating runway.

## 2. The Business Concept

### Customer problem

Specialty coffee customers often face several problems:

- Mexican coffee is available, but its regional identity and producer stories are not always presented clearly.
- Customers may not know how to select a coffee based on flavor, process, altitude, or brew method.
- Small producers have limited access to modern direct-to-consumer marketing and customer data.
- Many online coffee stores compete primarily on price instead of education, trust, and discovery.

### CafeAtlas solution

CafeAtlas will connect discovery, education, and commerce in one experience:

- Curated Mexican coffees with clear tasting and origin information.
- Producer, farm, and regional storytelling.
- Search and filtering by state, flavor, process, altitude, and producer.
- Personalized coffee recommendations and flavor memory over time.
- Web and mobile access.
- Subscriptions, bundles, gifts, and eventually wholesale offerings.
- Customer accounts with order history, fulfillment updates, notifications, and support workflows.

### Positioning

CafeAtlas should be positioned as:

> The digital home for discovering, learning about, and buying premium Mexican coffee.

The brand should emphasize authenticity, transparency, quality, education, and respect for producers. It should not compete primarily as the lowest-priced coffee seller.

## 4. Products to Launch With

### Recommended launch catalog

Start with a narrow catalog of 4 to 6 products:

- 2 approachable coffees for new specialty coffee customers.
- 1 to 2 fruit-forward or distinctive coffees for enthusiasts.
- 1 medium-to-full body coffee for customers who prefer chocolate, caramel, and nutty notes.
- 1 rotating seasonal or limited-release coffee.

Each coffee should have:

- Producer and farm information.
- Mexican state and region.
- Variety and altitude where available.
- Processing method.
- Roast profile.
- Tasting notes written in understandable language.
- Recommended brew methods.
- Harvest or availability information.
- Lot, batch, and traceability information where available.

### Product formats

The initial format should be whole-bean coffee in 8 oz or 12 oz bags. Possible launch products:

- Single-origin coffee bags.
- Two-bag discovery bundles.
- Three-bag regional tasting sets.
- Limited-release microlots.
- Subscription boxes.
- Gift boxes with a brew guide and producer story.
- Ground coffee only as an optional service, because freshness and grind consistency add operational complexity.

### Suggested price architecture

These prices are planning ranges and should be validated against actual landed costs:

| Product | Suggested retail range |
| --- | ---: |
| Standard 8 oz bag | $16 - $20 |
| Standard 12 oz bag | $20 - $28 |
| Premium microlot | $28 - $40 |
| Two-bag discovery bundle | $42 - $56 |
| Three-bag tasting set | $60 - $82 |
| Monthly subscription | $42 - $75 plus shipping |
| Gift box | $65 - $120 |

The final price must be based on actual product, packaging, freight, payment, fulfillment, and customer acquisition costs. Premium pricing is appropriate only when quality, traceability, presentation, and service support it.

## 5. Who We Need to Contact

### Supply-side contacts

The first outreach should target:

- Mexican coffee producers and producer cooperatives.
- Farm owners and regional associations.
- Specialty coffee importers already handling Mexican coffee in the United States.
- US specialty roasters that can roast and fulfill under a partnership arrangement.
- Green coffee exporters and logistics providers in Mexico.
- Packaging suppliers for resealable, valve-equipped coffee bags.
- Third-party logistics providers or fulfillment warehouses.

### Recommended initial sourcing strategy

For the first launch, the lowest-risk path is to work with a US-based importer or specialty roaster that already understands Mexican coffee logistics. This allows CafeAtlas to validate customer demand without immediately taking on every requirement of importing, customs, warehousing, roasting, and compliance.

Once demand is proven, CafeAtlas can increase direct relationships with producers and negotiate better pricing, exclusive lots, and more direct producer support.

### Questions for every potential supplier

- Are you selling roasted coffee or green coffee?
- What is the minimum order quantity?
- What is the wholesale price at 50, 100, and 500 units?
- What harvest, lot, farm, and producer information is available?
- Can you provide consistent supply for at least six months?
- What is the expected lead time?
- What quality control and sample process do you use?
- What packaging and labeling support is available?
- Can you support private label, co-branding, or fulfillment?
- What payment terms are available?
- Who handles export, import, customs, insurance, and delivery?
- How are producers paid, and can the relationship be communicated transparently?

### Product quality requirements

Before placing a meaningful order, CafeAtlas should obtain samples, establish a tasting rubric, document quality standards, and approve a written product specification. At minimum, the business should record roast date, origin, lot, producer, process, defects, sensory profile, and expected shelf life.

## 6. What Is Needed to Start

### Legal and business setup

- Form the appropriate business entity and ownership agreement.
- Establish partner responsibilities, decision rights, capital contributions, and exit terms in writing.
- Obtain tax registrations and a business bank account.
- Obtain appropriate general liability and product liability insurance.
- Prepare supplier, fulfillment, contractor, and customer terms.
- Establish a privacy policy, terms of service, return policy, and subscription terms.
- Confirm food labeling, sales-tax, shipping, and import requirements with qualified professionals.

Legal and tax requirements vary by location. They should be confirmed with a business attorney and CPA before taking public orders.

### Product and operations

- Approve the first 4 to 6 coffees.
- Secure samples and production pricing.
- Select packaging and labels.
- Decide whether coffee is roasted by CafeAtlas, a partner roaster, or an existing supplier.
- Select fulfillment, shipping, and returns procedures.
- Create inventory receiving and quality-control checklists.
- Establish customer support response standards.

### Technology and security

- Move Stripe from test mode to live mode only after end-to-end testing.
- Configure production Supabase, PostgreSQL, domains, email, and backups.
- Add error monitoring, uptime monitoring, structured logs, and alerting.
- Protect secrets and verify that no production keys are stored in source control.
- Complete payment, webhook, authentication, access-control, and database backup tests.
- Test web and mobile flows on real devices and common browsers.

### Marketing and content

- Produce professional product and producer photography.
- Write origin stories and brew guides.
- Create an email list before launch.
- Build a launch calendar for social content, tastings, and partner outreach.
- Prepare a referral and repeat-purchase program.
- Identify coffee educators, cafes, chefs, and creators who can introduce the brand to the right audience.

## 8. Unit Economics

The business should approve every product and marketing decision against contribution margin.

### Illustrative average order

Example two-bag order:

| Item | Example amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Customer order value | $52.00 |
| Coffee and packaging | -$18.00 |
| Shipping and fulfillment subsidy | -$9.00 |
| Payment processing | -$1.85 |
| Average acquisition/offer cost | -$5.00 |
| **Estimated contribution after variable costs** | **$18.15** |

This example produces an approximate 35% contribution margin. Actual margins will vary by supplier, shipping zone, discounting, subscription retention, and advertising performance.

### Metrics that must be tracked monthly

- Average order value.
- Gross margin by product.
- Contribution margin by order.
- Customer acquisition cost.
- Repeat purchase rate at 30, 60, and 90 days.
- Subscription retention and churn.
- Refund and return rate.
- Inventory turnover.
- Email conversion rate.
- Website conversion rate.
- Customer support cost per order.

If a product cannot produce acceptable contribution after fulfillment and payment costs, it should be repriced, bundled, renegotiated, or removed.

## 9. Revenue Needed to Start and Break Even

Revenue is not the same as profit. The business needs enough revenue to cover variable costs, fixed operating costs, taxes, owner compensation, and reinvestment.

### Planning assumptions

- Average order value: $52.
- Contribution after product, packaging, shipping, payment, and average acquisition cost: approximately 35%.
- Initial fixed operating costs: approximately $8,000 per month.
- Monthly fixed costs include software, insurance, accounting, support, content, basic marketing, and administrative expenses. Owner compensation may be additional.

### Basic break-even

At a 35% contribution margin and $8,000 in monthly fixed costs:

- Required monthly contribution: $8,000.
- Required monthly revenue: approximately $22,900.
- Required monthly orders at a $52 average order: approximately 440.
- Required annual revenue before owner compensation: approximately $275,000.

### More realistic operating target

To pay an owner modest compensation, maintain inventory, handle unexpected expenses, and continue marketing, the business should target:

- $30,000 to $40,000 in monthly revenue.
- $360,000 to $480,000 in annual revenue.
- Approximately 580 to 770 orders per month at a $52 average order.

This is why the initial goal should not be simply “make sales.” The goal should be repeatable, profitable sales with enough margin to support the operation.

## 11. Go-to-Market Plan

### Phase 1: Validate supply and economics, weeks 1 to 4

- Interview at least 10 producers, importers, roasters, or distributors.
- Obtain samples for 8 to 12 candidate coffees.
- Select the initial 4 to 6 coffees.
- Confirm landed cost, minimum orders, lead times, packaging, and fulfillment.
- Establish the legal entity, partner agreement, insurance, and business banking.
- Recruit a small group of coffee testers.

### Phase 2: Prepare the launch, weeks 5 to 8

- Finalize product pages, producer stories, images, and brew guides.
- Complete live Stripe, Supabase, email, tax, shipping, and monitoring configuration.
- Run test orders from payment through fulfillment and customer notification.
- Create customer support and returns procedures.
- Build an email waitlist and launch content library.

### Phase 3: Controlled launch, weeks 9 to 12

- Launch to a limited audience first.
- Cap inventory until fulfillment quality is proven.
- Collect customer feedback and tasting responses.
- Measure conversion, average order value, product margin, shipping cost, and repeat purchase intent.
- Fix operational problems before increasing advertising spend.

### Phase 4: Scale after proof

Scale only after the company can demonstrate:

- Reliable fulfillment.
- Positive contribution margin.
- Acceptable refund and return rates.
- Repeat purchase behavior.
- Accurate inventory records.
- Supplier reliability.
- Customer acquisition economics that do not depend entirely on discounts.

## 13. Risks and How to Reduce Them

### Inventory risk

Large upfront purchases can trap cash in slow-moving coffee. Start with small lots, limited releases, and reorder based on sales data.

### Import and supply risk

Direct importing adds customs, compliance, freight, currency, and timing risk. Use a qualified importer or roaster first, then build direct capability deliberately.

### Margin risk

Shipping, discounts, and paid advertising can eliminate apparent product margin. Review contribution margin by order, not just retail markup.

### Customer acquisition risk

Paid advertising can become expensive before brand awareness exists. Build email, referrals, educational content, tastings, and partnerships alongside paid marketing.

### Product freshness risk

Coffee quality declines with time. Manage roast dates, inventory age, forecast accuracy, and fulfillment speed.

### Partnership risk

An informal partnership creates avoidable conflict. Write down capital contributions, ownership, responsibilities, compensation, voting rights, intellectual property, and what happens if one partner leaves.

### Technology and security risk

Payments and customer accounts require production-grade security. Use least-privilege access, protected secrets, backups, monitoring, dependency updates, and a documented incident process.

## 14. The Partnership Proposal

### Recommended initial capital request

Seek an initial commitment of $75,000 to $125,000, released against milestones rather than spent all at once.

Suggested release structure:

- First release: $15,000 to $25,000 for legal setup, sampling, suppliers, content, and launch preparation.
- Second release: $25,000 to $40,000 after product economics and supplier agreements are approved.
- Third release: remaining capital after the launch meets agreed quality and sales milestones.

### Suggested launch milestones

- Signed partner and supplier agreements.
- Four to six approved products with documented costs.
- Production policies, returns policy, and fulfillment plan.
- Live payment and customer-account testing completed.
- First 100 paid customer orders delivered successfully.
- Contribution margin at or above the approved target.
- Repeat purchase and customer feedback data collected.

### What success looks like after 12 months

- $250,000 to $350,000 in revenue.
- 4 to 6 strong launch coffees plus controlled seasonal releases.
- Reliable supplier and fulfillment relationships.
- A growing email list and repeat customer base.
- A functioning subscription or bundle offer.
- Clear evidence of which products and stories create repeat demand.
- A defensible CafeAtlas brand and technology platform.

## 15. Decision Needed From the Partner

The immediate decision is not whether CafeAtlas can become a large company. The immediate decision is whether the partners want to fund and operate a disciplined 90-day commercial validation.

The proposed next actions are:

1. Confirm the partnership structure, responsibilities, and initial capital range.
2. Contact producers, importers, and roasters using the questions in this proposal.
3. Build a product cost sheet for every candidate coffee.
4. Select the first 4 to 6 products.
5. Complete legal, insurance, tax, shipping, and payment requirements.
6. Run a controlled launch and review the numbers every month.

CafeAtlas should earn the right to scale. If customers repeatedly purchase the coffee, the unit economics are positive, and operations remain reliable, the business can expand into subscriptions, gifts, wholesale, events, producer partnerships, and additional regional products.
